# Proposal and Quote Drafter — User Guide

Learn how to turn rough discovery notes into a polished client proposal in minutes.

---

## Quick Start

Here's the fastest way to get your first proposal:

1. Copy your raw notes from the discovery call (bullet points, free text, shorthand — anything)
2. Say: "Write a client proposal" and paste your notes
3. Get back a full proposal with scope, three pricing packages, a cover letter, and a review checklist

**Result:** A client-ready proposal document you can copy, refine, and send.

**Time:** ~3 minutes

---

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Sending a Proposal After a Discovery Call

**Situation:**
You are a freelance consultant. You just finished a 45-minute discovery call with a new prospect. You took rough notes during the call — a mix of bullet points and shorthand — and now you need to send a formal proposal within 24 hours. Writing it from scratch would take 2–3 hours.

**What to do:**

1. Open your notes from the call
   - Find whatever you wrote: bullet points, voice memos transcribed, Notion snippets, anything
   - Do a quick pass to make sure client name and core problem are visible

2. Trigger the skill by saying: "Write a client proposal"
   - Paste all your notes at once — no need to clean them up first
   - The skill will extract what it needs and flag what's missing

3. Review the generated proposal
   - Check the **Executive Summary** — does it capture the client's problem accurately?
   - Check **Scope / Deliverables** — are all discussed items included?
   - Check **Pricing Packages** — does the Standard tier match your intended quote?

4. Work through the Human Review Checklist
   - Fill in any `[FILL IN]` placeholders
   - Adjust pricing if you used `[PRICE]` placeholders
   - Verify the client name is spelled correctly

5. Copy the Cover Letter and Proposal, paste into your email client, and send

**Expected result:**

You receive:
- A full proposal structured into 5 sections (Summary, Scope, Timeline, Pricing, Next Steps)
- Three pricing tiers ready to discuss
- An email-ready cover letter (copy-paste into your email, no formatting needed)
- A review checklist with 4–5 specific items to verify

Instead of spending 2–3 hours writing from scratch, you spend 15 minutes reviewing and adjusting. The proposal is professional, structured, and consistent with your other proposals.

**Why this works:** The skill does the structural work — you bring the knowledge from the call. Your notes, however messy, contain everything needed; the skill extracts and organizes it.

### Scenario 2: Preparing a Quote with No Budget Discussed

**Situation:**
You are a web developer. A potential client reached out via email with a vague brief: they want a landing page and CRM integration, but no budget was mentioned. You want to send three pricing options so the client can choose their level of investment without you having to ask awkward money questions upfront.

**What to do:**

1. Paste the client's email or your own notes about the request
   - Even a few sentences are enough to get started
   - Include any specific details they mentioned (CRM system, number of pages, etc.)

2. Say: "Draft proposal and quote from my notes"
   - Paste the content
   - The skill will build pricing packages with `[PRICE]` placeholders since no budget was mentioned

3. Fill in the pricing tiers
   - The skill shows you a Basic / Standard / Premium structure
   - Add your actual rates based on your estimates
   - Use the tip the skill provides if you're unsure how to set rates

4. Adjust the cover letter
   - The generated cover letter references the three tiers naturally
   - Adjust the opening line to match how you know this client

**Expected result:**

You receive a proposal with three pricing tiers using placeholder prices. After adding your rates, you have a clean, professional quote that positions you with options — giving the client control over scope and budget, and reducing the friction of budget conversations.

**Why this works:** Presenting three tiers is a proven sales technique — clients focus on "which option" rather than "whether to hire." The skill builds this structure automatically from your notes.

### Scenario 3: Bilingual Proposal for an International Client

**Situation:**
You are a translator working with clients from both Russian-speaking and international markets. A Russian client sent you a brief in Russian, but they want the proposal delivered in English for their investors. You need the full proposal in English but want to work from your Russian notes.

**What to do:**

1. Paste the Russian brief as-is
   - No need to translate the input first

2. Say: "Write a client proposal in English" (specify the language explicitly)
   - The skill detects the language mismatch and switches to English output

3. Review the English proposal
   - Check that any Russian terms (names, company names, product names) are correctly transliterated or translated
   - Verify the tone is appropriate for international business context

4. Use the Russian-language version for your own reference
   - If you want an internal Russian version too, say: "Напиши предложение клиенту" and paste the same notes

**Expected result:**

You receive a full English-language proposal suitable for international investor review, generated from your Russian working notes. The skill handles the language switch cleanly and flags any terms that may need your attention.

**Why this works:** The skill detects your requested output language and overrides the auto-detection from notes. You can use the same notes to generate proposals in both languages without re-entering anything.

## Tips

### Tip 1: The Messier Your Notes, The More the Skill Helps

Don't clean up your notes before pasting. The skill is designed to work with exactly what you captured during the call — abbreviations, half-sentences, quick shorthand. Cleaning up takes time and is exactly the work the skill does for you. Paste raw; review the output.

**Pro tip:** If you record calls, paste the auto-transcription directly. The skill handles conversation transcripts well — it filters out small talk and focuses on the project details.

### Tip 2: Use `[FILL IN]` Flags as Your Review Checklist

When the skill can't find certain information in your notes (client name, timeline, specific deliverables), it generates a `[FILL IN]` placeholder and flags it. Treat these as your personal follow-up list: the things you need to either ask the client or fill in from memory. Don't send the proposal until all `[FILL IN]` items are resolved.

**Pro tip:** If you see many `[FILL IN]` flags, consider scheduling a quick 15-minute follow-up call with the client to clarify before sending the proposal.

### Tip 3: Always Work Through the Human Review Checklist

The ⚠️ Human Review Checklist is not optional decoration — it exists because AI-drafted proposals fail most often on pricing errors, client name typos, and unrealistic timelines. Before clicking send, check every item. The pricing check alone can save you from quoting the wrong rate.

**Pro tip:** Add the checklist to your standard proposal workflow. After every proposal — not just AI-generated ones — run through: pricing correct? client name correct? timeline realistic? legal terms match your agreement?
