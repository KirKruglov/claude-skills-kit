# Win/Loss Debrief Writer — User Guide

Learn how to turn closed deal notes into a structured debrief with decision drivers, objections, competitive context, and a trend row.

---

## Quick Start

Here's the fastest way to get a debrief:

1. Copy your notes from a recently closed or lost deal
2. Say: "Win loss debrief" and paste the notes
3. Get back a structured markdown document with five sections and a trend row

**Result:** A structured debrief you can share with your sales team and copy into a deal log.

**Time:** ~3 minutes

---

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Debriefing a Lost Deal Before the Details Fade

**Situation:**

You are a sales manager at a B2B SaaS company. You just got off a call where a $60k deal fell through — the prospect chose a competitor. It's been two hours since the meeting. You want to capture exactly what happened, understand why you lost, and note what to change before your next similar deal. You also want a one-line entry for your monthly win/loss spreadsheet.

**What to do:**

1. Write down your raw notes from the call (or copy them from your CRM / meeting notes)
   - Include what the prospect said about their decision
   - Note the competitor's name if mentioned
   - Add the deal size and prospect's industry if you know them

2. Say: "Win loss debrief" and paste your notes
   - No need to structure your notes — free-form text works fine

3. Review the debrief sections
   - **Decision Drivers** — see what actually drove the buyer's choice
   - **Objections Encountered** — check which were resolved vs. still open
   - **Competitive Context** — see where the competitor outpositoned you and what to address in future battlecards

4. Copy the Trend Row
   - Paste it into your win/loss spreadsheet as a new row
   - Add a date if the skill couldn't extract one

**Expected result:**

You receive a structured document with:
- The 1–2 factors that actually drove the decision (not just the excuses)
- A table of objections showing which you handled and which slipped through
- A battlecard gap note if the competitor had an angle you couldn't counter
- A concrete "Fix next time" recommendation — specific enough to act on

You capture the institutional knowledge from this loss before it fades, and your spreadsheet gains a new data point for pattern analysis next quarter.

**Why this works:** Most lost deals are never analyzed — the notes live in the CRM but nobody reads them. This skill forces the debrief into a format that's shareable and pattern-ready in under 3 minutes.

### Scenario 2: Building a Deal Pattern Log Over Time

**Situation:**

You are a founder doing founder-led sales. Over the past month, you've closed 4 deals and lost 3. You've noticed you keep losing to one particular competitor but aren't sure why. You want to debrief all 7 deals and build a simple pattern log to inform your next sales cycle — no CRM, just a Google Sheet.

**What to do:**

1. For each deal, gather your raw notes
   - Pull from email threads, your notes app, Notion, or Slack messages
   - Even 3–5 sentences per deal will produce a usable debrief

2. Run the skill once per deal
   - Say: "Win loss debrief" and paste the notes for the first deal
   - Repeat for each deal in sequence (paste one at a time for full analysis)

3. Collect the Trend Rows
   - After each debrief, copy the Trend Row line
   - Paste all 7 lines into a Google Sheet (one row per deal)
   - Your sheet now has: Date | Customer | Outcome | Size | Competitor | Top Driver | Key Fix

4. Look for patterns across the 7 rows
   - Which competitor appears most often in losses?
   - Which objection recurs most?
   - Which "Repeat" element shows up in most wins?

**Expected result:**

After 7 debriefs (about 20 minutes total), you have:
- 7 structured debrief documents you can share or reference
- A 7-row spreadsheet showing deal patterns at a glance
- A concrete answer to "why do we keep losing to that competitor"

You identify that losses to the main competitor all share one battlecard gap — a specific feature you don't have. That becomes your next product and sales priority.

**Why this works:** Single-deal debriefs are useful; a log of debriefs is strategic intelligence. The Trend Row format is designed to be machine-readable (copy-paste into a spreadsheet) without requiring any tools except a text editor.

### Scenario 3: Preparing a Win/Loss Review for the Sales Team

**Situation:**

You are a sales team lead. Every Friday, you run a 30-minute team review of the week's closed and lost deals. You want each salesperson to come prepared with a structured debrief so the meeting focuses on patterns and coaching — not on reconstructing what happened deal by deal.

**What to do:**

1. Before the Friday meeting, ask each salesperson to run a debrief
   - They paste their deal notes and get a structured document in 2–3 minutes
   - They share the document link or paste it into your shared notes

2. You aggregate the debrief outputs before the meeting
   - Collect the Trend Rows from each debrief
   - Paste them into the weekly deal log spreadsheet

3. During the meeting, focus on the debrief outputs — not raw notes
   - Use the Decision Drivers to spot patterns ("Three losses this week all mention timeline — is this becoming systemic?")
   - Use Competitive Context to update battlecards in real time
   - Use Repeat/Fix to generate concrete coaching actions

**Expected result:**

Each team member arrives with a structured, shareable debrief. The meeting spends zero time reconstructing deals and 100% of the time analyzing patterns, updating battlecards, and coaching on specific objection-handling improvements.

**Why this works:** Debriefs only work if they happen consistently and in a standard format. This skill removes the friction — it takes 3 minutes to produce a debrief that would otherwise take 20 minutes to write manually (or never happen at all).

## Tips

### Tip 1: Include the Deal Outcome Explicitly

The skill can infer outcome from context ("they chose a competitor" = LOST, "signed yesterday" = WON), but adding it explicitly — "Outcome: LOST" or "We won this deal" — helps the skill immediately focus the Repeat/Fix section on the right direction.

**Pro tip:** If you're not sure of the outcome (e.g., prospect went quiet), the skill generates both variants and asks you to confirm. This is useful for tentative situations.

### Tip 2: Mention the Competitor by Name, Even if You Know Little

Even if you only know the competitor's name and nothing else, include it. The skill will note "limited competitor context — name only" and flag it as a battlecard research opportunity. Over time, your trend log will show which competitors appear most often, even if individual debriefs are sparse.

**Pro tip:** After several losses to the same competitor, run a pattern search across your trend rows — even a simple keyword search in a spreadsheet surfaces their recurring edge.

### Tip 3: Use the Trend Row as Your Deal Log, Not a Separate CRM Entry

The Trend Row format (`Date | Customer | WON/LOST | Size | Competitor | Top Driver | Key Fix`) is designed for a simple spreadsheet, not a CRM. Copy it into a Google Sheet tab called "Deal Log" and let it grow. After 20–30 entries, you'll have enough data to spot statistically meaningful patterns without any analytics tool.

**Pro tip:** Add a "Quarter" column to your spreadsheet to filter by time period. Quarterly win/loss patterns are usually more actionable than weekly snapshots.
