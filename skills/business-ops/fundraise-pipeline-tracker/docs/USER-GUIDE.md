# Fundraise Pipeline Tracker — User Guide

Learn how to use Fundraise Pipeline Tracker to turn raw investor notes into a structured, actionable pipeline during a fundraising round.

---

## Quick Start

Here's the fastest way to get your pipeline:

1. Paste your investor notes — any format works (names, statuses, meeting summaries)
2. Say: "Fundraise pipeline" and paste the notes
3. Get back a tiered pipeline table, per-investor detail, and a prioritised Needs Action list

**Result:** A structured investor pipeline you can use immediately to plan your outreach.

**Time:** ~2 minutes

---

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Weekly Pipeline Review Before Investor Calls

**Situation:**

You are a founder in the middle of a seed round. It's Monday morning and you have three investor calls this week. Your tracking notes are scattered across email drafts and a Google Doc that hasn't been updated in a week. You need to know who is in what stage, who needs materials sent, and who has gone quiet — before you start making calls.

**What to do:**

1. Open your notes (email, Google Doc, voice memos — anything you have)
   - Copy-paste everything about each investor into one message
   - Don't worry about formatting; bullet points, paragraphs, and mixed languages all work

2. Trigger the skill by saying: "Show me my investor pipeline status"
   - Paste all your investor notes in one message
   - Add your round context at the top if you have it: "Seed round, $1M target, aiming to close in September"

3. Review the Pipeline Table
   - Check Tier 1 investors first — these are your hottest leads; prioritise any overdue actions
   - Review Tier 2 investors for pending follow-ups
   - Note any Tier 3 investors that may need to be re-engaged or dropped

4. Work from the Needs Action list
   - Send any overdue materials first (cap table, data room, financial model)
   - Write follow-up emails to investors who've gone silent
   - Use the "Why Us" and "Feedback" fields to customise each message

**Expected result:**

You receive a three-section markdown document:
- **Pipeline Table:** All investors in one view with tier, status, next step, and feedback
- **Per-Investor Detail:** Full breakdown per investor for your prep notes before each call
- **Needs Action:** Overdue items and silent investors, sorted by urgency

You walk into each call knowing exactly where you stand with that investor and what they care about.

**Why this works:** Instead of re-reading scattered notes before each call, you have one clean structured view. You spend 10 minutes reviewing instead of 45 minutes reconstructing context.

### Scenario 2: Rebuilding a Stale Pipeline After a Break

**Situation:**

You are a founder who took two weeks off from active fundraising to focus on product. Your investor pipeline spreadsheet is completely out of date — some conversations stalled, some investors went quiet, and you've lost track of who's where. You have meeting notes and email snippets but nothing structured. You need to quickly re-establish who's still active before you can restart outreach.

**What to do:**

1. Gather everything you have on each investor
   - Scrape your email sent folder for investor threads from the last 4–6 weeks
   - Copy the last message you sent or received from each investor
   - Include any notes you jotted down after meetings (even informal ones)

2. Trigger the skill: "Track my investors"
   - Paste all the raw material — email snippets, meeting notes, voice-to-text, anything
   - The skill handles incomplete or inconsistent information; it will flag what's missing

3. Check the "Gone Silent" section first
   - These are investors who haven't responded in 14+ days
   - Prioritise re-engagement for anyone in Tier 1 or Tier 2 who's gone quiet
   - Use the last known "Why Us" and "Feedback" fields to craft a relevant check-in message

4. Update the pipeline as you reach out
   - After each conversation, paste updated notes and re-run the skill to refresh the view
   - Each run replaces the old output with a fresh snapshot

**Expected result:**

You receive a rebuilt pipeline that shows:
- Which investors are still warm (Tier 1 / Tier 2 with recent activity)
- Which investors have gone silent and by how long
- What each investor's last known status, feedback, and next step was

You have a clear starting point for re-engagement without spending an hour piecing together the history yourself.

**Why this works:** The skill processes messy, incomplete notes and still produces a usable structure. Even partial information is enough to get a directional view of where your fundraise stands.

### Scenario 3: Preparing a Status Update for a Co-Founder or Advisor

**Situation:**

You are a founder who handles investor relations. Your technical co-founder needs to understand the fundraising status before a board call. You want to share a clean overview — not dump raw notes — so they can answer questions from the board about who's in pipeline and what the next milestones are.

**What to do:**

1. Compile your most recent investor notes into a single message
   - Include everyone in pipeline regardless of tier — the skill will structure them
   - Add the round context: stage, target, timeline, lead status if you have a lead

2. Say: "Fundraise pipeline" and paste the compiled notes

3. Share the output directly
   - Copy the Pipeline Table section for a high-level view
   - Include the Needs Action list to show what's happening next
   - Add the Per-Investor Detail only if the co-founder needs depth on specific names

4. Walk through it together
   - Use the tiering and status fields as talking points
   - The "Feedback" column surfaces what investors are saying — useful context for a board call

**Expected result:**

You share a clean markdown document that your co-founder can read in 2 minutes:
- Who's in the pipeline and at what stage
- What's hot (Tier 1), what needs nurturing (Tier 2), what's long-shot (Tier 3)
- What actions are pending and who's gone quiet

**Why this works:** Instead of walking through scattered notes verbally, you have a structured document that communicates the full picture without needing a 30-minute catch-up conversation.

## Tips

### Tip 1: Include Dates When You Have Them

The skill uses dates to detect investors who've gone silent (no response in 14+ days). If you include the date of your last contact with each investor ("met Jun 12", "sent email Jun 13"), the skill will automatically flag anyone who hasn't responded. Without dates, it can only flag investors you've explicitly marked as non-responsive.

**Pro tip:** Even approximate dates work ("met last week", "intro in early June"). The skill interprets them and notes any uncertainty.

### Tip 2: Run It Weekly, Not Just Once

The skill works best as a recurring snapshot. Run it at the start of each week with updated notes, and you'll always have a current view of your pipeline. The previous output is just a reference — each new run generates a fresh document based on what you paste.

**Pro tip:** Keep a running "investor notes" document during the week. Drop in notes after each meeting or email exchange. On Monday, paste the whole document and re-run the skill.

### Tip 3: Use the Feedback Column to Personalise Follow-Ups

The skill extracts what each investor said — their concerns, what resonated, their questions. Use this when writing follow-up emails. A message that references "the churn concern you raised" or "the market size question from last time" is far more effective than a generic check-in.

**Pro tip:** If the Feedback column is blank for an investor, that's a signal — either the meeting didn't surface real objections (good sign) or you didn't capture them. Go back to your notes and add any specifics you remember.
