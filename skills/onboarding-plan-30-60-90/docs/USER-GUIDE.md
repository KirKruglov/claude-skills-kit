# Onboarding Plan 30-60-90 User Guide

Learn how to use Onboarding Plan 30-60-90 to generate structured first-90-days plans for new hires from the manager's perspective.

---

## Quick Start

Here's the fastest way to get an onboarding plan:

1. Note the new hire's role, level, and 3–5 key priorities for the role
2. Say: "Create a 30-60-90 day plan for [role]" and paste your notes
3. Get back a markdown document with three phase sections: Orient, Contribute, Own

**Result:** A structured plan with goals, contacts, milestones, and a success metric per phase — ready to share with the new hire.

**Time:** ~3 minutes

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Onboarding a First-Time Hire (No HR Team)

**Situation:**
You are a team lead at a 15-person startup. You've just hired your first Product Designer — no HR team, no formal onboarding program. Your designer starts in two weeks and you need to give them a clear, structured plan for the first 90 days so they can hit the ground running and you don't have to micromanage every step.

**What to do:**

1. Jot down the essentials before triggering the skill
   - Role: Product Designer, Level: Mid-level
   - Work format: Hybrid (3 days office)
   - Priorities: learn Figma component library, understand design system, shadow user research sessions, participate in product sprint cycles, learn the brand voice and visual guidelines

2. Trigger the skill by saying: "Create a 30-60-90 day plan for a mid-level Product Designer"
   - Paste or describe your priorities list
   - Include any optional context (start date, key teammates they'll work with)

3. Review the generated plan
   - Check **Phase 1 Key Contacts** — make sure the listed roles exist in your team and rename them if needed
   - Check **Learning Milestones** — verify they reflect your actual design stack and processes
   - Adjust the **Success Metric** for Phase 1 if the proposed one doesn't match your expectations

4. Share the plan with the new hire
   - Copy the markdown into Notion, Confluence, or a shared doc
   - Walk through it with the new hire on Day 1 so they understand what "success" looks like at each phase

**Expected result:**

You receive a 3-phase plan covering:
- Phase 1 (Orient): meeting the team, learning core design tools, understanding the design system
- Phase 2 (Contribute): owning a component or small feature design end-to-end
- Phase 3 (Own): driving design decisions independently and proposing system improvements

The new hire has a clear roadmap, and you have concrete success metrics to discuss at 30-, 60-, and 90-day check-ins.

**Why this works:** Instead of improvising onboarding on the fly (and either under-supporting or over-managing), you hand the new hire a clear, structured plan on Day 1. The success metrics give you an easy framework for your check-in conversations.

### Scenario 2: Onboarding a Senior Leadership Hire

**Situation:**
You are a VP of Engineering who has just hired a new Engineering Manager (Senior-level). The EM will lead a team of 8 engineers and own the roadmap for two product areas. This hire requires a different kind of onboarding — less about learning tools and more about building relationships, understanding org dynamics, and establishing credibility quickly.

**What to do:**

1. Prepare input for the leadership context
   - Role: Engineering Manager, Level: Senior
   - Work format: Hybrid
   - Priorities: map team strengths and growth areas, build relationships with product and design leads, understand existing roadmap commitments, learn the incident response process, understand the hiring pipeline for the team

2. Say: "Create a 30-60-90 day onboarding plan for a Senior Engineering Manager"
   - Paste your priorities and include: "This is a leadership hire — they'll own a team of 8 engineers"
   - The skill will apply a leadership adjustment: Phase 1 focuses on stakeholder mapping, not just tool learning

3. Review the leadership-adjusted plan
   - Phase 1 contacts will include cross-functional leads and key stakeholders, not just teammates
   - Phase 2 will include a "quick-win" goal (identify and deliver one visible improvement)
   - Phase 3 will include team strategy or hiring decisions

4. Share and schedule check-ins
   - Walk through the plan with the new EM on Day 1
   - Schedule 30-, 60-, and 90-day check-ins with the success metrics as the agenda framework

**Expected result:**

You receive a leadership-adjusted plan where:
- Phase 1 maps the org landscape and key stakeholders, not just tools
- Phase 2 drives quick wins and cross-functional relationship building
- Phase 3 covers strategic ownership (roadmap planning, team development, hiring)

The new EM knows what success looks like at each phase and has a relationship-building roadmap to navigate the org.

**Why this works:** Leadership hires fail most often because of misaligned expectations and slow stakeholder trust-building — not lack of technical knowledge. This plan front-loads relationship mapping and makes success criteria explicit from Day 1.

### Scenario 3: Remote-First Hire in a Distributed Team

**Situation:**
You manage a distributed team of 12 people across 4 time zones. You've just hired a Senior Data Analyst who will work fully remotely. The challenge: most of your onboarding is built around in-person interactions that won't work here. You need an async-first onboarding plan that helps the new hire build relationships and get up to speed without relying on hallway conversations.

**What to do:**

1. Specify the remote context clearly in your input
   - Role: Senior Data Analyst, Level: Senior
   - Work format: Remote
   - Priorities: learn data warehouse structure, understand product analytics framework, get familiar with BI tooling (Looker), understand business KPIs and reporting cadence, shadow stakeholder review meetings (async recordings)

2. Say: "Create a 30-60-90 day plan for a remote Senior Data Analyst"
   - The skill will apply the remote adjustment: contacts become async-first, milestones include async comms tools

3. Review for async relevance
   - Check that Key Contacts suggest virtual/async meeting modes (Slack intros, Loom walkthroughs, recorded demos)
   - Confirm Learning Milestones include your team's async documentation tools (Notion, Confluence, recorded meeting archives)

4. Add links to async resources
   - After generating the plan, add direct links to Notion docs, Loom recordings, or onboarding wikis in the Learning Milestones section
   - This turns the plan into a clickable async onboarding portal

**Expected result:**

You receive a remote-adjusted plan where:
- Key Contacts include virtual intro modes (Slack, async video, virtual coffee)
- Learning Milestones include async documentation standards and tools
- Phase 2 includes ownership of a recurring async deliverable (e.g., weekly data digest)

The new hire can onboard independently with clear async touchpoints at each phase.

**Why this works:** Remote onboarding fails when async context isn't baked into the plan. The remote adjustment flips the default (in-person contact → async equivalent) automatically, so you don't have to remember to do it manually.

## Tips

### Tip 1: Give Role Priorities, Not a Job Description

The more specific your priorities, the more tailored the milestones. Instead of pasting the full job description, distill it to 3–5 must-learn areas. For example: "learn our sprint process, understand customer segments A and B, master Salesforce CRM, shadow quota planning cycle, understand the QBR format."

**Pro tip:** If you're unsure what the priorities should be, ask yourself: "What would this person need to know to pass a 30-day check-in without needing hand-holding?" That list becomes your priorities.

### Tip 2: Adjust Key Contacts to Real Names

The generated contacts will use generic roles ("Engineering Lead", "Cross-functional Partner"). Before sharing the plan with the new hire, replace generic role names with the actual names of the people they'll meet. This makes the plan immediately actionable on Day 1.

### Tip 3: Use Success Metrics as Your Check-In Agenda

Each phase ends with a Success Metric — a concrete, measurable indicator. Use these directly as the agenda for your 30-, 60-, and 90-day check-in conversations. "At the 30-day mark: can you explain our product roadmap process to someone outside the team?" is a much more productive check-in frame than "How's it going so far?"
