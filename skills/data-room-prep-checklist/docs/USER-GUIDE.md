# Data Room Prep Checklist — User Guide

Learn how to use Data Room Prep Checklist to get your fundraising documents organized before due diligence.

---

## Quick Start

Here's the fastest way to get your checklist:

1. Know your round stage (pre-seed / seed / Series A) and business model (SaaS / marketplace / etc.)
2. Say: "Data room checklist for my seed round, B2B SaaS" and hit send
3. Get back a prioritized checklist, a folder structure, and a gap list

**Result:** A ready-to-act preparation package covering all six investor due-diligence categories.

**Time:** ~3 minutes

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Preparing for Your First VC Due Diligence

**Situation:**
You are a first-time founder who just received a term sheet from a seed-stage VC. The firm sent a due-diligence request list, but it's long and unclear which items are truly required vs. optional. You need to understand what to prioritize in the next 2 weeks and where to put everything.

**What to do:**

1. Collect basic information about your round:
   - Stage: seed
   - Business model: B2B SaaS
   - Investor type: VC

2. Trigger the skill by saying: "Data room checklist for my seed round, B2B SaaS, VC investor"
   - That's all you need to provide upfront
   - The skill will generate a full checklist with priority labels

3. Review the checklist by priority:
   - Start with all **[Must-have]** items — these are deal-breakers if missing
   - Schedule **[Expected]** items for week two
   - Gather **[Nice-to-have]** items only if you have time

4. Set up the folder structure:
   - Copy the recommended folder hierarchy from the output
   - Create it in Google Drive or Dropbox
   - Name the root folder: `Data Room — [Your Company] — Seed 2026`

5. Share the gap list with your lawyer:
   - Forward the "What you're likely missing" section to your legal counsel
   - Ask them to confirm all documents are countersigned originals, not drafts

**Expected result:**

You receive:
- A prioritized 30-item checklist across 6 categories with Must-have / Expected / Nice-to-have labels
- A ready-to-use folder structure for your data room platform of choice
- A gap list with the 3–5 items most commonly missing at seed stage and 2–3 predictable investor follow-up questions

You know exactly what to collect, in what order, and where to store it — without guessing or over-preparing.

**Why this works:** The checklist is calibrated to seed stage and B2B SaaS specifically, so you don't waste time preparing Series B-level legal packages or missing the basics that a seed VC always checks.

### Scenario 2: Auditing an Existing Data Room Before a Formal Process

**Situation:**
You are a repeat founder preparing for a Series A. You already have a data room from your seed round, but it's 18 months old. The term sheet is signed, and the lead investor is starting formal due diligence in 10 days. You need to quickly identify what's outdated or missing before the investor team opens the room.

**What to do:**

1. Export or copy your current data room document list:
   - Go through your Google Drive or Notion data room
   - List the files you have (document names, not content)

2. Trigger the skill by saying: "Series A data room audit, B2B SaaS, VC. Here's what I have:" and paste your list

3. Review the delta output:
   - **✓ Covered** — nothing to do for now
   - **⚠ Partial** — document exists but may be outdated or incomplete (e.g., cap table from 18 months ago)
   - **✗ Missing** — needs to be collected before investor access

4. Prioritize your gaps:
   - Address all ✗ Missing items with Must-have label first
   - Update all ⚠ Partial items (especially cap table, financial model, and bank statements)
   - Add ✗ Missing items with Expected label in the remaining days

5. Review the investor follow-up questions:
   - The "What the investor will ask next" section predicts their first 2–4 data requests
   - Prepare those proactively so you're not scrambling after they open the room

**Expected result:**

You receive a delta summary showing the exact gap between your current data room and Series A requirements. You have a clear action plan for the next 10 days — no guessing, no over-preparing.

**Why this works:** Instead of rebuilding the data room from scratch (which wastes time on items you already have), you get a targeted list of what's missing and what needs updating. Investors notice when a data room is well-organized; it signals operational maturity.

### Scenario 3: Hardware / Deep-Tech Startup

**Situation:**
You are a hardware startup founder preparing for seed due diligence. You know the standard checklist tools are designed for SaaS, so the output often misses critical items specific to your industry — like IP filing status, manufacturing agreements, and regulatory certifications.

**What to do:**

1. Trigger the skill by saying: "Data room checklist for seed round, hardware / deep-tech startup, VC investor"

2. Review hardware-specific additions in the output:
   - **IP Portfolio & Patents:** Provisional patents filed, freedom-to-operate analysis, inventor assignment agreements
   - **Manufacturing:** Contract manufacturer agreements, unit economics at scale (per-unit BOM), production timeline
   - **Regulatory:** CE / FCC / FDA certification status (or roadmap), any pending filings

3. Note the gap list items unique to hardware:
   - Patent filing dates and expiration
   - Signed contracts with contract manufacturers
   - Current BOM (Bill of Materials) with cost breakdown

4. Set up the hardware-extended folder structure:
   - The output includes `/IP & Patents` and `/Manufacturing & Regulatory` in addition to the six standard categories

**Expected result:**

You receive a hardware-specific checklist that won't miss the IP and regulatory items that SaaS-focused tools overlook. Your data room is investor-ready for a hardware-aware seed fund.

**Why this works:** Seed VCs investing in hardware expect IP clarity and manufacturing viability from day one. Missing these items is the most common reason hardware due diligence stalls — not financials.

## Tips

### Tip 1: Always Confirm "Fully Diluted" Cap Table

When the checklist says "cap table (fully diluted)," it means the table should show every equity stake — including issued shares, unissued option pool, outstanding SAFEs or convertible notes converted to equity, and any promised but unissued grants. A cap table that shows only issued shares will fail the investor's first review. Ask your lawyer or CFO to confirm the fully diluted view before you share it.

**Pro tip:** Include a post-money projection column showing ownership percentages after the current round closes (assuming all SAFEs and notes convert). Investors expect to see this at seed and Series A.

### Tip 2: Countersigned Originals Only — No Drafts

Every legal document in your data room should be a fully executed (countersigned) original, not a draft or a template. A common mistake is uploading a form shareholders agreement without all parties' signatures. The investor's legal team will flag unsigned documents immediately, and it can delay or derail closing.

**Pro tip:** Color-code your folder structure: green = countersigned, yellow = pending signature, red = not started. Share only the green items with the investor; keep yellow and red in a separate internal folder.

### Tip 3: Use the Gap List as Your Weekly Checklist

Don't treat the gap list as a one-time output. At the start of each week during due diligence, re-read the "What the investor will ask next" section and confirm you have the answers ready. Investors typically send a follow-up data request list 3–5 days after opening the room. If you've prepared for the predictable questions in advance, you'll respond in hours instead of days — which signals operational readiness and accelerates closing.

**Pro tip:** Paste the "What the investor will ask next" list into your weekly prep email to your lawyer and CFO so everyone is aligned on what's coming.
