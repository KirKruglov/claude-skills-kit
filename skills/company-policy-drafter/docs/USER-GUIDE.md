# Company Policy Drafter User Guide

Learn how to use Company Policy Drafter to create handbook-ready policies for your team — with or without a lawyer on staff.

---

## Quick Start

Here's the fastest way to get a policy:

1. Say: "Draft a PTO policy" (or remote work, expenses, AI-usage)
2. Add your key rules: "20 days per year, requires 2 weeks' notice"
3. Get a bilingual (EN + RU) policy document with a legal review reminder

**Result:** A structured five-section policy ready for your employee handbook.

**Time:** ~3 minutes

---

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Writing Your First Remote Work Policy

**Situation:**
You are a startup founder with a fully remote team of 12. You've been operating informally for two years, but a new investor asked you to document your remote work practices. You need a professional remote work policy for your employee handbook — quickly, without hiring a lawyer just for this.

**What to do:**

1. Trigger the skill by saying: "Draft a remote work policy"
   - Add any rules you already have in mind: "Employees must be available during core hours 10 AM–3 PM EST. Home office equipment is provided by the company. Team members must use a VPN when accessing company systems."

2. Review the generated document
   - Check the **Rules** section — confirm your core hours and equipment policy are correctly included
   - Review the **Procedure** section — adjust the approval flow to match how your team actually operates
   - Fill in any `[SPECIFY VALUE]` placeholders (e.g., effective date, specific reimbursement amounts)

3. Act on the legal flag
   - The document includes ⚠️ "Legal review required" — share with a labor attorney or HR consultant before publishing
   - This is especially important if you have team members in multiple countries

4. Publish to your handbook
   - Once reviewed, paste both the EN and RU blocks into your Notion / Confluence / Google Doc handbook page

**Expected result:**

You receive a complete bilingual remote work policy with:
- Purpose (why the policy exists)
- Scope (applies to all remote employees)
- Rules (core hours, equipment, VPN, availability expectations)
- Procedure (how to request WFH changes, report issues)
- Responsibility (who enforces, who approves exceptions)

You go from "no policy" to "ready for legal review" in under 5 minutes. The investor sees a professional HR foundation.

**Why this works:** Instead of copying a generic template from the internet (and not knowing which laws apply), you get a structured document with clear placeholders and an explicit reminder to consult a lawyer for your jurisdiction.

### Scenario 2: Updating an Expenses Policy After Company Growth

**Situation:**
You are an operations manager at a 50-person SaaS company. Your expenses policy hasn't been updated in 3 years. The old policy: "Employees submit receipts; manager approves." Now you want to add per-category limits (meals: $50, travel: $250/night, equipment: $500/year) and a new digital submission process. You need to send an updated policy to all employees and explain what changed.

**What to do:**

1. Trigger the skill in update mode:
   - Say: "Update our expenses policy"
   - Paste the old policy text
   - Describe the new rules: "Add meal cap $50, hotel cap $250/night, equipment budget $500/year. Receipts must now be submitted via Expensify within 30 days."

2. Review the updated policy
   - Check the **Rules** section — confirm all three caps appear correctly
   - Review the **Procedure** section — make sure Expensify and the 30-day deadline are included
   - Check the **Change Summary** section — verify it lists what changed (caps added, new tool) and who to notify

3. Fill in placeholders
   - Effective date: set to the first of next month
   - Notification deadline: typically 2 weeks before effective date

4. Distribute to your team
   - Use the Change Summary to write a brief announcement email
   - Attach the full updated policy as a PDF or link to the handbook page

**Expected result:**

You receive:
- Updated bilingual policy with all new rules and limits included
- Change summary with three fields: what changed, who to notify (all employees + finance team), notification deadline

Your announcement email writes itself from the change summary. Employees know exactly what's new.

**Why this works:** The change-summary mode saves you the step of manually diffing the old and new versions. The structured output makes it easy to communicate the update professionally.

### Scenario 3: Adding an AI-Usage Policy Before Your Team Starts Using AI Tools

**Situation:**
You are a team lead at a 30-person consultancy. Several team members have started using ChatGPT and Claude for client work. You want to create a formal AI-usage policy before this becomes a compliance issue — especially around client confidentiality. You have no existing policy.

**What to do:**

1. Say: "Create an AI-usage policy"
   - Add your key constraints: "Employees can use AI for internal drafts and brainstorming. Client data and proprietary information must not be entered into external AI tools. All AI-generated content must be reviewed by a human before sending to clients."

2. Review the output
   - Check the **Rules** section — ensure the client data restriction is explicit and unambiguous
   - Check the **Procedure** section — add any internal approval steps or tool whitelist you want to include
   - Review the **Responsibility** section — confirm who is accountable for violations

3. Address the legal flag
   - AI-usage policies intersect with client contracts and data privacy law — the legal review here is especially important
   - Consider having your client contracts reviewed for AI-use clauses at the same time

4. Roll out to the team
   - Schedule a short team meeting to walk through the new policy
   - Send the bilingual document so all team members (EN and RU speakers) can read in their preferred language

**Expected result:**

You receive a complete AI-usage policy that:
- Permits AI for internal, non-client-facing work
- Prohibits inputting client data into external AI tools
- Requires human review of AI-generated content before delivery
- Includes all required sections in both EN and RU

Your team has clear guidance in writing, and you've proactively addressed a growing compliance risk.

**Why this works:** AI-usage policies are new territory with no widely accepted standard. The skill gives you a solid, practical starting point — with the legal flag reminding you that this area is evolving rapidly and professional review is essential.

## Tips

### Tip 1: Fill in Specific Values for a Stronger Policy

The more specific parameters you provide, the less placeholder text you'll need to fill in later. Instead of "draft a PTO policy," say "draft a PTO policy: 20 days per year, no carryover, 2 weeks advance notice required, manager approval needed." You'll get a ready-to-publish document instead of a template with blanks.

**Pro tip:** Keep a "policy parameters" note in Notion or a Google Doc where you store your key rules. Copy-paste from there when triggering this skill.

### Tip 2: Always Take the Legal Flag Seriously

The ⚠️ "Legal review required" flag is mandatory for good reason — labor laws vary dramatically by country, state, and even city. A PTO policy legal in Germany is different from one in California, which is different from one in Russia. Before publishing any policy generated by this skill, share it with a labor attorney or HR consultant familiar with your jurisdiction.

**Pro tip:** If you use a PEO (Professional Employer Organization) like Deel, Remote, or Gusto, they often include policy review in their service. Ask them to review before rolling out.

### Tip 3: Use the Change Summary to Write Your Announcement Email

When updating an existing policy, the change-summary section is ready-made content for your internal announcement. Take the "What changed" and "Who to notify" fields and paste them directly into an email or Slack message: "We've updated our expenses policy. Here's what changed: [paste]. Effective [date]."

**Pro tip:** Send the policy update announcement at least 2 weeks before the effective date. This gives employees time to ask questions and adjust their behavior before the new rules apply.
