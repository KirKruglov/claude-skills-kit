# North Star Metric Auditor — User Guide

Learn how to audit your current North Star Metric and find better alternatives in minutes.

---

## Quick Start

Here's the fastest path to an audit:

1. Note your current NSM (e.g., "Monthly Active Users")
2. Say: "Audit north star metric" and describe your product in 1–2 sentences
3. Get a criteria table, a verdict (Strong / Acceptable / Weak), and 2–3 alternatives with trade-offs

**Result:** A structured audit report with a single actionable recommendation — keep or replace your NSM.

**Time:** ~5 minutes

---

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Pre-Strategy-Review NSM Check

**Situation:**
You are a Head of Product at a B2C subscription fitness app. Quarterly strategy review is in three days. The CEO is asking whether the team is measuring the right thing — you've been using DAU as your NSM for a year, but the team keeps gaming it with cheap push notifications. You need a structured, defensible audit to bring to the exec team.

**What to do:**

1. Trigger the skill: "Audit north star metric"
   - Provide your NSM: "Daily Active Users"
   - Add your business model: "B2C subscription fitness app; monthly subscription revenue; core value = workout completion"

2. Review the criteria table
   - Focus on the **Team Actionability** criterion — this is likely where DAU scores poorly when it's being gamed
   - Note which criteria are Weak — these point to the structural problem with DAU

3. Take the top alternative to the exec meeting
   - Copy the top-ranked alternative candidate with its justification
   - Use it as your proposal: "Here's why we should switch from DAU to [alternative], and here's how we measure it"

**Expected result:**

You receive an audit table with 4 criteria ratings (likely Weak on Leading Indicator), an overall Acceptable or Weak verdict, and 2–3 alternatives (e.g., "Weekly workout completions per user", "30-day activation rate"). Each alternative explains exactly why it fixes DAU's weakest points.

**Why this works:** Instead of a subjective debate about which metric is "better," you bring a structured framework with 4 objective criteria. Leadership can see exactly where DAU fails, not just that it feels imperfect.

### Scenario 2: Choosing Your First NSM After PMF

**Situation:**
You are a founder of a B2B SaaS tool that helps HR teams automate employee onboarding. The product just hit product-market fit and the team is growing — you need a shared North Star Metric to align product, growth, and engineering. You have three candidates but don't know which one to choose.

**What to do:**

1. Provide all three candidates at once
   - Say: "Help me choose a better north star metric" and list them: "Option 1: Seats onboarded, Option 2: Employees completing first onboarding flow, Option 3: HR managers returning weekly"
   - Add your business model: "B2B SaaS; per-seat subscription; core value = employee time-to-productivity"

2. Review the ranked comparison
   - The skill audits each candidate on all 4 criteria and ranks them by verdict
   - Look at which candidate scores Strong on the most criteria

3. Use the output to align your leadership team
   - Share the comparison table at your next all-hands
   - Use the recommendation as a starting point for discussion — not a final decree

**Expected result:**

Three individual audits ranked by verdict. "Employees completing first onboarding flow" likely comes out strongest — it directly captures time-to-productivity (core value), predicts seat expansion, and is directly actionable through product improvements.

**Why this works:** Comparing three candidates simultaneously surfaces trade-offs that are invisible when you think about each metric in isolation.

---

### Scenario 3: Aligning on One NSM After a Merger

**Situation:**
Your company recently merged with a competitor. Both teams use different NSMs, and leadership wants to pick one for the combined entity. You need to evaluate both metrics on equal footing and recommend which one — or a new alternative — should become the unified NSM.

**What to do:**

1. Run the skill twice — once per NSM
   - First: "Audit north star metric" for NSM #1, using the merged company's business model
   - Second: same for NSM #2 with the same business model description

2. Compare the two verdicts side by side
   - Note which criteria each NSM scores Strong vs. Weak on
   - Look for the metric that covers both teams' core value

3. If neither is Strong, use the alternatives
   - The alternative candidates from either audit may surface a better unified NSM

**Expected result:**

Two separate audit reports on equal footing. If NSM #1 scores Acceptable and NSM #2 scores Weak, you have a defensible, data-backed recommendation for leadership.

**Why this works:** A structured comparison removes the political default of "whichever team is bigger wins." Both metrics are evaluated against the same 4 criteria, neutrally.

## Tips

### Tip 1: Be Specific About Your Business Model

The quality of alternative candidates depends entirely on how well you describe your business model. "B2C app" produces generic suggestions. "B2C freemium language-learning app; annual Pro subscription revenue; core value = vocabulary retention through daily 5-minute practice" produces targeted alternatives.

**Pro tip:** Always include your revenue model and describe the moment a user receives core value — not just the product category.

### Tip 2: Financial Metrics Are a Valid Starting Point

If you're currently using revenue, MRR, or GMV as your NSM, don't skip the audit — run it. The skill flags financial metrics as Weak and uses the audit process to surface a leading indicator that predicts your revenue. This is often the fastest path to finding the right NSM.

### Tip 3: The Verdict Is a Framework, Not the Final Word

Ratings are based on the business model description you provide. If that description is incomplete, some criteria may miss important nuances. Use the audit to start the conversation, then stress-test the alternatives with your analytics or growth lead before committing to a new NSM.

**Pro tip:** After the audit, ask your analytics lead: "Does this match what we see in the data?" The skill provides the framework; your team provides the ground truth.
